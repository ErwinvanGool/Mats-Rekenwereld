# Build Experience UX Rubric

## Purpose
This rubric is used by the Child Playtester agent to evaluate the building experience
of "Mats Rekenwereld" from the perspective of a 6-year-old child.

It ensures that every review is consistent, comparable, and actionable.
The Build Implementer agent uses the scores and feedback to prioritize improvements.

## How to use this rubric
- Score each item from 1 to 5
- Be honest and specific
- Always explain the score with a one-line reason
- Reference specific files and line numbers where possible
- Think like a 6-year-old child, not like a developer

## Scoring scale

| Score | Meaning | Description |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | Poor | A 6-year-old would be confused, frustrated, or unable to use this |
| 2 | Weak | A 6-year-old might figure it out but would not enjoy it |
| 3 | Okay | It works but is not fun, clear, or rewarding enough |
| 4 | Good | A 6-year-old would enjoy this and understand it easily |
| 5 | Excellent | A 6-year-old would love this, find it exciting, and want to do it again |

---

## Category 1: Clarity

These items evaluate whether the child immediately understands what to do
on the build screen without reading any text.

1. Is it immediately clear what the child should do when entering the build screen?
   - Look for: visual cues, arrows, highlights, animations that guide attention
   - Fail if: the child would stare at the screen not knowing what to tap or drag

2. Is the build example/reference clearly visible and easy to understand?
   - Look for: a clear target image or model shown prominently on screen
   - Fail if: the reference is too small, hidden, or abstract

3. Are visual cues sufficient to guide building without any text?
   - Look for: icons, colors, shapes, shadows, outlines, glowing hints
   - Fail if: the child would need to read instructions to understand the flow

4. Is it clear which blocks are available and how many the child has?
   - Look for: a visible block inventory with count, color-coded blocks
   - Fail if: the child cannot see what they have earned or what they can use

5. Is it clear when a build is finished?
   - Look for: completion animation, visual change, celebration trigger
   - Fail if: the child would not realize they completed the build

---

## Category 2: Fun and engagement

These items evaluate whether the building experience is enjoyable and
keeps a 6-year-old engaged for a full play session.

6. Does dragging blocks feel satisfying and playful?
   - Look for: smooth movement, responsive feedback, visual snap effect
   - Fail if: dragging feels laggy, unresponsive, or too precise

7. Is there enough visual and audio feedback during building?
   - Look for: sounds on drag, sounds on drop, color changes, sparkles
   - Fail if: placing a block feels silent and unnoticed

8. Would a 6-year-old want to keep building for at least 5 minutes?
   - Look for: variety, progression, small rewards during building
   - Fail if: building feels repetitive or boring after a few blocks

9. Does the building experience feel like play, not like homework?
   - Look for: toy-like visuals, cheerful colors, playful interactions
   - Fail if: the screen looks like a form, grid, or worksheet

10. Is there a sense of creative freedom within the guided experience?
    - Look for: some flexibility in block placement, room for personal choices
    - Fail if: building is purely mechanical with no room for play

---

## Category 3: Reward satisfaction

These items evaluate whether the reward moments during and after
building feel motivating and exciting.

11. Does earning a block after a correct answer feel rewarding?
    - Look for: animation, sound, visual block appearing in inventory
    - Fail if: the block just silently appears or the child does not notice

12. Is the connection between math and building clear and motivating?
    - Look for: visual link between answering correctly and gaining a block
    - Fail if: math and building feel like two disconnected activities

13. Does completing a build feel like a real celebration?
    - Look for: fireworks, confetti, sticker reward, sound fanfare, animation
    - Fail if: completion is just a text message or a simple screen change

14. Are streak bonuses and stickers visible and exciting?
    - Look for: bonus block animation, sticker popup, collection display
    - Fail if: bonuses happen silently in the background

15. Does the child want to start the next build after completing one?
    - Look for: teaser of next build, return to world map with new unlock visible
    - Fail if: after completion there is no clear path forward

---

## Category 4: Frustration risk

These items evaluate whether the building experience could cause
confusion, frustration, or being stuck.

16. What happens when a block is placed wrong? Is it gentle and encouraging?
    - Look for: friendly animation, block bounces back, gentle sound, no penalty
    - Fail if: wrong placements cause error messages, harsh sounds, or lost blocks

17. Can the child get stuck with no way forward?
    - Look for: always a clear next action, help available, back button visible
    - Fail if: there is a dead end where the child cannot proceed

18. Is there too much complexity for a 6-year-old?
    - Look for: simple grid, few block types, clear target, minimal choices
    - Fail if: too many options, complex rotations, tiny placement areas

19. Can the child accidentally lose progress or leave the build screen?
    - Look for: confirmation before leaving, auto-save, protected navigation
    - Fail if: a single accidental tap causes loss of work

20. Is there always a visible way to go back to the world map?
    - Look for: large, icon-based back/home button always visible
    - Fail if: the child is trapped on a screen with no obvious exit

---

## Category 5: Touch and interaction

These items evaluate whether the building interactions work well
on an iPad with touch gestures for small fingers.

21. Are drag targets large enough for small fingers on iPad?
    - Look for: blocks at least 44x44px, preferably 60x60px or larger
    - Fail if: blocks are small, hard to grab, or require precise tapping

22. Does drag-and-drop work smoothly on touch devices?
    - Look for: responsive touch events, no delay, clear pickup and drop feedback
    - Fail if: dragging is jerky, unresponsive, or blocks snap to wrong positions

23. Are there any interactions that require precision a child cannot achieve?
    - Look for: generous drop zones, forgiving placement areas, snap-to-grid
    - Fail if: pixel-perfect placement is needed to place a block correctly

24. Is scrolling handled correctly so it does not interfere with dragging?
    - Look for: prevented default scroll during drag, no accidental page movement
    - Fail if: the page scrolls when the child tries to drag a block

25. Do all interactive elements have sufficient spacing between them?
    - Look for: comfortable gaps between buttons and draggable items
    - Fail if: elements are too close together, causing accidental taps

---

## Category 6: Visual design

These items evaluate whether the build screen looks appealing,
child-friendly, and appropriate for a 6-year-old.

26. Is the build screen colorful, cheerful, and toy-like?
    - Look for: bright colors, rounded shapes, playful style, toy-brick inspiration
    - Fail if: the screen looks dull, corporate, or too minimal

27. Are there unnecessary text labels that could be replaced with icons?
    - Look for: icon-based navigation, visual counters, picture-based instructions
    - Fail if: there are text labels, sentences, or written instructions

28. Is the layout clean and uncluttered?
    - Look for: clear visual hierarchy, focused content, breathing room
    - Fail if: too many elements on screen, visual overload, confusing layout

29. Are colors and shapes consistent with the rest of the game?
    - Look for: same color palette, same button style, same visual language
    - Fail if: the build screen looks like a different app

30. Are animations smooth, delightful, and not distracting?
    - Look for: subtle transitions, satisfying micro-animations, purposeful motion
    - Fail if: animations are jerky, too fast, too slow, or distracting from gameplay

---

## Score interpretation

After scoring all 30 items, calculate the overall average score.

| Average score | Verdict | Meaning |
|---------------|---------|---------|
| Below 2.0 | Needs major work | The building experience has fundamental problems that would confuse or frustrate a 6-year-old |
| 2.0 to 3.0 | Getting better | The basics work but there are significant gaps in fun, clarity, or usability |
| 3.0 to 4.0 | Almost there | The experience is decent but needs polish to feel truly delightful |
| 4.0 or above | Fun enough to ship | A 6-year-old would enjoy this and want to play again |

The target for version 1 is an overall score of 3.5 or higher.
The long-term goal is 4.0 or higher.

## How to compare reviews across iterations

To track progress effectively:

1. Always save each review in docs/reviews/ as review-1.md, review-2.md, etc.
2. Include the review number in the Review Summary
3. Compare the overall score with the previous review
4. Compare category averages to identify which areas improved most
5. Track whether the top issues from the previous review were addressed
6. Note any new issues that appeared after changes were made
7. Look for score regressions that might indicate unintended side effects

A healthy improvement cycle looks like:
- Overall score increases by 0.3 to 0.5 per iteration
- The lowest-scoring category gets the most improvement
- No category drops by more than 0.3 between iterations
- Previous top issues are visibly addressed
- The verdict moves up one level every 1 to 2 iterations

If the score stalls or declines, consider:
- Reviewing whether changes actually addressed the right issues
- Checking for regressions in areas that were previously fine
- Asking the developer to test manually on an iPad
- Simplifying the building mechanic rather than adding complexity

---

## Important notes

- This rubric focuses exclusively on the building experience
- The math screen, start screen, world map, and parent settings have their own evaluation criteria
- If the building experience depends on another system (e.g., rewards, navigation), note the dependency but do not fully review that other system
- Always think from the child's perspective, not the developer's perspective
- A beautiful implementation that a child cannot use scores low
- A simple implementation that a child loves scores high
